## `rational-exponent`

> A count above a number does not have to be whole. The bottom of a fractional count says which root to take, and the top says what power to raise it to.

**Prerequisites.** `radical-simplify`, `zero-negative-exponent`

| Framework | Code | 19 TAC | Depth | Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSN.RN.A.2` | — | core | Rewrite expressions involving radicals and rational exponents using the properties of exponents. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSN.RN.A.1` | — | supporting | Explain how the definition of the meaning of rational exponents follows from extending the properties of integer exponents to those values, allowing for a notation for radicals in terms of rational exponents. |
| TEKS | `A.11(B)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(11)(B)` | core | simplify numeric and algebraic expressions using the laws of exponents, including integral and rational exponents. |
| TEKS | `A.11(A)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(11)(A)` | supporting | simplify numerical radical expressions involving square roots; and |

- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSN.RN.A.1 — what is NOT claimed.** Rewriting a radical as a rational exponent, and back, is core and is what the gate tests. EXPLAINING why the definition follows from the laws of integer exponents is not asked for: the bank marks a value, not an argument, and this unit ships no item whose answer is a sentence.
- **A.11(B) — what is NOT claimed.** The integral half of this expectation is not asked here. Every count on screen is a fraction, or a square root written as one. Whole-number and negative whole-number counts are the prerequisite zero-negative-exponent, and Level 3 claims them at core. This node adds the rational half.
- **A.11(A) — what is NOT claimed.** Simplifying a numerical square root is Level 4's CORE claim, on its own radical nodes, and this node does not restate it. What is claimed here is the bridge: the same simplification carried out under a fractional count, so a learner reads a count of one half and a square root as one idea rather than two. A radical whose radicand is not a whole number, and a sum of two unlike radicals, both belong to Level 4 and are not served here.

**What the gate proves.**

- *CCSS-M* — HSN.RN.A.2 is core. Every item shows a base with a fractional count above it, or a square root of a whole number, and the learner returns the exact value or the simplest radical. The checker TAKES NO ROOT: it raises the learner's own value to the bottom count and requires the result to equal the base raised to the top count, in exact arithmetic, so a generator that is wrong about its own root cannot make it agree. HSN.RN.A.1 is claimed at supporting for its rewriting clause only, and the caveat names the clause left alone.
- *TEKS* — A.11(B) — "simplify numeric and algebraic expressions using the laws of exponents, including integral and rational exponents" — is a sentence-for-sentence description of this node. Level 3 finished the integral half; this is the node that adds the rational half. The claim carried NO caveat and said both halves were on screen here, and that was wrong: every count this node prints is a fraction, or a square root written as one, and a count below zero appears only as the top of a fraction. The integral half is the prerequisite zero-negative-exponent, in Level 3, which claims it at core. npm run check:cases generates the items and reads them against the words of the expectation, which is how the sentence was found. A.11(A) is claimed at supporting rather than core, because Level 4's radical nodes own it: this node takes that skill as a PREREQUISITE and carries it only far enough to show that a count of one half IS a square root. The radical forms here take one square root of a whole number and simplify it to a whole coefficient and a square-free radicand, proved by squaring back in whole numbers.

**Process standards.** CCSS-M MP.2, MP.6, MP.7 · TEKS A.1(A), A.1(D), A.1(G)

**Misconceptions this node declares.** `exponent-becomes-a-factor`, `top-and-bottom-swapped`, `root-index-ignored`, `negative-count-makes-a-negative`, `base-rooted-and-count-kept`, `partial-rule`, `arith-slip`

**What the gate proves.**

**Misconceptions this node declares.** `boundary-strictness-ignored`, `side-chosen-from-the-rate`, `lean-not-turned-after-dividing`, `test-reading-substituted-backwards`, `equation-given-instead-of-a-lean`, `arith-slip`

## `write-system`

> One statement is not enough when two things are unknown. Two different statements about the same pair of unknowns pin both of them down at once.

**Prerequisites.** `point-slope-form`, `system-substitution`

| Framework | Code | 19 TAC | Depth | Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TEKS | `A.2(I)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(2)(I)` | supporting | write systems of two linear equations given a table of values, a graph, and a verbal description. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.CED.A.3` | — | supporting | Represent constraints by equations or inequalities, and by systems of equations and/or inequalities, and interpret solutions as viable or nonviable options in a modeling context. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.CED.A.2` | — | supporting | Create equations in two or more variables to represent relationships between quantities; graph equations on coordinate axes with labels and scales. |

- **A.2(I) — what is NOT claimed.** A pair of linear equations is written from two tables of values and from a verbal description. It is NOT written from a graph, because this unit draws none.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.CED.A.3 — what is NOT claimed.** Two constraints are represented by a system of equations, which is the middle clause. Representing a constraint by an inequality belongs to the previous node, and interpreting a solution as viable or nonviable in a modelling context is not tested.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.CED.A.2 — what is NOT claimed.** Two equations in two variables are created from two data sets, which is the first clause. Graphing them on coordinate axes is the second clause and is not met.

**What the gate proves.**

- *CCSS-M* — The existing system checker proves a SOLUTION and cannot prove that a written pair describes two data sets, so this node uses one that fits both printed tables exactly, refuses a pair of tables that fit the same line, and matches the learner's two rules against the two fits in either order. That last clause matters: a learner who writes the rules the other way round has written the same system, and being marked wrong for the order would be a defect of the marker.
- *TEKS* — A.2(I) — "write systems of two linear equations given a table of values, a graph, and a verbal description" — is served for two of its three representations, and the caveat names the third. The commonest wrong answer, reporting the pair of numbers where the pair of statements was asked for, is on the option list as a tagged misconception rather than as a random perturbation.

**Process standards.** CCSS-M MP.2, MP.4 · TEKS A.1(A), A.1(B), A.1(D)

**Misconceptions this node declares.** `two-conditions-merged`, `same-line-twice`, `letters-redefined-between-rows`, `counts-added-to-values`, `solved-instead-of-written`, `arith-slip`

## `system-graphically`

**Prerequisites.** `write-system`, `system-elimination`, `graph-linear`

| Framework | Code | 19 TAC | Depth | Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TEKS | `A.5(C)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(5)(C)` | core | solve systems of two linear equations with two variables for mathematical and real-world problems. |
| TEKS | `A.3(F)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(3)(F)` | supporting | graph systems of two linear equations in two variables on the coordinate plane and determine the solutions if they exist; |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.REI.C.6` | — | supporting | Solve systems of linear equations exactly and approximately (e.g., with graphs), focusing on pairs of linear equations in two variables. |

- **A.3(F) — what is NOT claimed.** The second half — "determine the solutions if they exist" — is core and is what the gate tests, through a checker that solves the printed pair by Cramer's rule in exact rationals and refuses a pair that does not meet at one point. GRAPHING the system on the coordinate plane is not met: each rule's own readings are printed in a table beside it, and the crossing is the row that appears in both, which is a table and not a plane.

- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSA.REI.D.11 — what is NOT claimed.** The crossing of two rules really is where one equals the other, and the table of values route the expectation names is the route this node takes. The EXPLANATION of why, and the cases where either rule is polynomial, rational, absolute value, exponential or logarithmic, are not tested: both rules here are straight.

**What the gate proves.**

- *CCSS-M* — Both Common Core claims are supporting, and both caveats name the drawing. The mathematics that IS proved is the mathematics a graph is usually used to approximate: the checker re-solves the printed pair exactly, and every worked line of the derivation has to be a statement that is true at the solution, which is what substitution, elimination and back-substitution all are.
- *TEKS* — A.5(C) — "solve systems of two linear equations with two variables for mathematical and real-world problems" — is core, and the situation forms carry the real-world half. A.3(F) is supporting and its caveat separates the two halves of the expectation by name. A.3(G), which asks a learner to estimate a solution GRAPHICALLY, is NOT claimed at all: nothing here is estimated and nothing here is drawn. The unit's declared gaps say so.

## `scatter-regression`

> Real readings never sit exactly on one line. The closest line is the one line that comes nearest to all of them at once, and it can be used to predict.

**Prerequisites.** `write-linear`, `slope-rate`, `rule-from-table`

| Framework | Code | 19 TAC | Depth | Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TEKS | `A.4(C)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(4)(C)` | supporting | write, with and without technology, linear functions that provide a reasonable fit to data to estimate solutions and make predictions for real-world problems. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.C.7` | — | supporting | Interpret the slope (rate of change) and the intercept (constant term) of a linear model in the context of the data. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.C` | — | supporting | Fit a linear function for a scatter plot that suggests a linear association. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.A` | — | supporting | Fit a function to the data; use functions fitted to data to solve problems in the context of the data. Use given functions or choose a function suggested by the context. Emphasize linear, quadratic, and exponential models. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6` | — | supporting | Represent data on two quantitative variables on a scatter plot, and describe how the variables are related. |

- **A.4(C) — what is NOT claimed.** This claim is supporting, not core. A.4(C) asks the learner to WRITE a linear function. No question here asks for one: all five forms take a single number as the answer, which is the rate, the starting value or one prediction. The prediction half is proved, because a value is estimated past the end of the readings in a real situation. The writing half is not.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.C.7 — what is NOT claimed.** This claim is supporting, not core. S-ID.C.7 asks the learner to INTERPRET the rate and the constant term in the context of the data. The answer surface here is a number keypad, so the learner returns those two numbers and never says what they mean. Working out the rate and the constant term of the closest line is proved. Interpreting them is not.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.C — what is NOT claimed.** A linear function IS fitted, exactly, to readings that suggest a linear association. The readings arrive as a printed table rather than as a SCATTER PLOT, because this unit draws none.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.A — what is NOT claimed.** A function is fitted to the data and used to solve problems in the context of the data, which is the first half. The emphasis on quadratic and exponential models is not met here: every model in this node is linear, and the exponential half lives in the exponential-model node.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6 — what is NOT claimed.** How the two measurements are related is described, through the exact rate of the fitted line. Representing them ON A SCATTER PLOT is not met.

**What the gate proves.**

- *CCSS-M* — HSS.ID.C.7 is SUPPORTING, and was core until a reading of the answer surface said otherwise: the rate and the constant term of the fitted line are both asked for, but every form is answered on a number keypad, so the learner computes them and never interprets them. The fit itself is EXACT LEAST SQUARES — the normal equations solved by Cramer’s rule in exact rationals, over the readings the learner is shown — and not an interpolation through two chosen readings, which is the commonest way a bank fakes this and is on the option list as a tagged misconception. The readings genuinely scatter: each table is built from a line plus a pattern of gaps whose sum is zero and whose sum against the inputs is zero, so the closest line is provably the line the table was built from, while no reading sits on it.
- *TEKS* — A.4(C) — "write, with and without technology, linear functions that provide a reasonable fit to data to estimate solutions and make predictions for real-world problems" — is SUPPORTING, and was core until the verb was read against the item bank. The estimating and predicting half is served: a value is returned at an input past the end of the data, in a situation. The writing half is not served at all, because every one of the five forms is answered with a single number and none of them asks for the function itself. The word "reasonable" is doing no work here, because the fit is exact.

**Process standards.** CCSS-M MP.4, MP.5, MP.6 · TEKS A.1(A), A.1(B), A.1(C)

**Misconceptions this node declares.** `first-and-last-joined`, `rate-from-one-pair`, `prediction-read-off-the-data`, `smallest-reading-called-the-start`, `prediction-stated-as-a-measurement`, `sign-flipped-only`, `arith-slip`

## `residual-and-fit`

> The gap between a real reading and the model's value is the residual. Gaps that keep the same sign along a run say the wrong shape of rule was chosen.

**Prerequisites.** `scatter-regression`, `quadratic-model`

| Framework | Code | 19 TAC | Depth | Expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.B` | — | supporting | Informally assess the fit of a function by plotting and analyzing residuals. |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.A` | — | supporting | Fit a function to the data; use functions fitted to data to solve problems in the context of the data. Use given functions or choose a function suggested by the context. Emphasize linear, quadratic, and exponential models. |
| TEKS | `A.4(C)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(4)(C)` | supporting | write, with and without technology, linear functions that provide a reasonable fit to data to estimate solutions and make predictions for real-world problems. |

- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.B — what is NOT claimed.** Residuals are ANALYSED: each one is the reading take away the fitted value, in that order, and a run of gaps that keeps one sign is printed back as the reason a straight rule is the wrong shape. PLOTTING them is not met, because this unit draws nothing.
- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSS.ID.B.6.A — what is NOT claimed.** A fitted function is used to solve problems in the context of the data, and the residuals say whether the function suggested by the context was the right one. The emphasis on quadratic and exponential models is not met: every model here is linear.
- **A.4(C) — what is NOT claimed.** The prediction half of the expectation is served, and the residual is what says whether the prediction is worth making. WRITING the linear function is the previous node's core claim, not this one's.

**What the gate proves.**

- *CCSS-M* — HSS.ID.B.6.B is the claim this node exists for, and it is claimed at supporting because the expectation asks for the residuals to be PLOTTED and this unit plots nothing. Everything else in it is met and is re-derived: the checker fits the line itself, subtracts, and returns the difference with its sign, so an answer that drops the sign or works the subtraction the other way round is wrong by arithmetic. A gap below zero and its own sign flip are the SAME NUMBER, so the bank tags only the one it can tell apart, and does not claim to diagnose the other.
- *TEKS* — Texas puts residual analysis inside A.8(B), which asks for a QUADRATIC model fitted using technology. This unit fits no quadratic and uses no technology, so A.8(B) is NOT CLAIMED and the declared gaps say so. What is claimed is A.4(C) at supporting, for the prediction half that a residual is evidence about. The prerequisite on Level 4's quadratic-model node is deliberate, and is the honest half of this: a learner cannot call a straight rule the WRONG SHAPE until some other shape exists for them, and a run of gaps on one side is what points at it.

**Process standards.** CCSS-M MP.3, MP.5, MP.6 · TEKS A.1(A), A.1(F), A.1(G)

**Misconceptions this node declares.** `residual-sign-flipped`, `single-largest-gap-used`, `curved-data-kept-linear`, `residual-taken-at-the-wrong-row`, `prediction-stated-as-a-measurement`, `arith-slip`

|---|---|---|---|---|

| TEKS | `A.9(B)` | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(9)(B)` | core | interpret the meaning of the values of a and b in exponential functions of the form f(x) = ab^x in real-world problems; |
| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSF.LE.B.5` | — | core | Interpret the parameters in a linear or exponential function in terms of a context. |

| CCSS-M | `CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSF.LE.A.2` | — | supporting | Construct linear and exponential functions, including arithmetic and geometric sequences, given a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input-output pairs (include reading these from a table). |

- **CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.HSF.LE.A.2 — what is NOT claimed.** An exponential function is constructed from a table of input-output pairs, which the expectation names outright. Constructing one from a GRAPH is not tested, and the linear half of this expectation belongs to Level 2.

A.9(B) is core too, through the situation forms, which ask what the starting amount and the factor MEAN in the story. A.9(A) — the domain and range of an exponential function, represented using inequalities — is NOT CLAIMED: this unit writes no inequality about a domain or a range, and the declared gaps say so.
