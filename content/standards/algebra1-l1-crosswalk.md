## 2. Skill by skill, with the TEKS text in full

A code is not a citation. Each block below gives the knowledge-and-skills statement that scopes the expectation, the expectation word for word, the legal cite into 19 Texas Administrative Code, and the named item forms that carry it.

### 1. `var-meaning`

> A letter is a placeholder for a number that has not been named yet — not a label, not an object, not its own position in the alphabet.

**Requires:** nothing — this is an entry point  
**Representations the gate demands:** symbolic, contextual  
**CCSS:** 6.EE.A.2.A, 6.EE.B.6, HSA.SSE.A.1.A · practices MP.2 MP.6  
**TEKS process standards:** A.1(A), A.1(D)

| TEKS | Depth | 19 TAC citation | Knowledge and skills statement | Student expectation | Evidence in this skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6.6(C)** | core | `19 TAC §111.26(b)(6)(C)` | The student applies mathematical process standards to use multiple representations to describe algebraic relationships. The student is expected to: | represent a given situation using verbal descriptions, tables, graphs, and equations in the form y = kx or y = x + b. | `vm-context`, `vm-choose`, `vm-compose`, `vm-partWhole`, `vm-share` |
| **7.7** | supporting | `19 TAC §111.27(b)(7)` | The student applies mathematical process standards to represent linear relationships using multiple representations. | The student is expected to represent linear relationships using verbal descriptions, tables, graphs, and equations that simplify to the form y = mx + b. | `vm-table` |
| **A.12(B)** | introduced | `19 TAC §111.39(c)(12)(B)` | The student applies the mathematical process standards and algebraic methods to write, solve, analyze, and evaluate equations, relations, and functions. The student is expected to: | evaluate functions, expressed in function notation, given one or more elements in their domains; | `vm-groups`, `vm-position`, `vm-table`, `vm-compose`, `vm-partWhole` |

**6.6(C) — what is and is not claimed.** Claimed narrowly and on purpose. 6.6(C) names only the forms y = kx and y = x + b, so it is cited only where the item records exactly one of those two. The grouping forms record y = kx — a situation such as `k stands, each carrying 2 mills, each mill returning x watts`. The part-whole form records y = x + b, which is the other half of the standard and had no evidence at all until it existed. The share form records y = kx with k a unit fraction, which is the same equation written over a bar. Any richer rule — anything of the form mx + b — is cited at 7.7 instead, where TEKS actually puts it. Getting this boundary wrong is the commonest way a vendor alignment sheet stops being true.

**7.7 — what is and is not claimed.** This knowledge-and-skills statement has no lettered student expectations; the expectation is the sentence itself. It is the home of every Level 1 rule of the form mx + b that appears as a table, a trace or a verbal description — which is most of them, and which is why 6.6(C) is claimed so narrowly above. Level 1 supplies the table or the trace and asks the learner to read or complete it; the learner is not asked to construct one, so the producing half belongs to a later level.

**A.12(B) — what is and is not claimed.** FUNCTION NOTATION DOES NOT APPEAR AT LEVEL 1. What is met is the substitution half — a rule is evaluated at a given element of its domain, including negative elements and two-variable rules. The f(x) wrapper that this expectation names is a later level's job, and this entry is INTRODUCED for exactly that reason. The 2012 TEKS for grades 6–8 contain no student expectation whose object is evaluating an algebraic expression at a value; it lives inside 7.7's multiple representations and inside this expectation's function notation, and nowhere else. That is a gap in the standard, not a gap in this alignment, and it is why eval-expr carries no TEKS entry at depth `core`.

**Why these codes.** TEKS has no grade 6–8 student expectation that says "a letter stands for a number" on its own; the idea is presupposed by 6.6(C) and named only at Algebra I inside A.12(B). Level 1 therefore claims 6.6(C) for the forms that record a described situation in algebra, and A.12(B) as INTRODUCED for the forms that evaluate a rule at a given value — with function notation absent, which is why the depth is introduced and not core.

**CCSS mapping note.** 6.EE.A.2.A and 6.EE.B.6 are covered by the contextual and choose-the-expression forms, where the learner must produce or select the expression that records a described situation. HSA.SSE.A.1.A is covered by the coefficient-reading forms, which ask what the number written against a letter means.

## 5. Where the alignment is imperfect, and why

An alignment sheet with no stated limits is a sales document. These are the places where TEKS and this level do not sit flush, written down so a reviewer does not have to find them.

### `eval-expr` — No TEKS student expectation at depth `core`.

The 2012 TEKS name no student expectation whose object is "evaluate an algebraic expression at a given value" anywhere in grades 6–8. The skill is exercised inside 7.7 (a linear relationship shown as a table, a graph and a verbal description) and named only at A.12(B), where it is wrapped in function notation that Level 1 does not use. Level 1 therefore claims 7.7 as SUPPORTING and A.12(B) as INTRODUCED, and claims nothing as core.

*Resolution:* Reported rather than papered over. The CCSS side of this node is core (6.EE.A.2.C), so the skill is not unaligned — it is a place where the two frameworks genuinely differ.

### `var-meaning` — 6.6(C) is claimed only for the y = kx forms.

6.6(C) names the forms y = kx and y = x + b and nothing else. Level 1 items whose rule is mx + b are cited at 7.7 instead. A vendor sheet that cited 6.6(C) for every table in this level would be overclaiming.

*Resolution:* Split deliberately; see the caveat on each entry.

### Across the level — Two of the seven Algebra I process standards are marked `partial`.

A.1(C) asks the student to SELECT tools; Level 1 chooses the balance beam or the area model for them. A.1(G) asks for justification in written or oral communication; Level 1 asks the learner to decide which of two chains of reasoning is sound, and does not collect prose.

*Resolution:* Marked `met: "partial"` in processStandards with the missing half named. Not counted as full coverage anywhere in this document or in the game.

### `order-ops` — No §111.39 content expectation of any kind.

The 2012 TEKS place order of operations at 6.7(A) and never restate it in Algebra I. There is no Algebra I student expectation this skill could honestly be cited against — A.11(B) is the laws of exponents, which is a different thing, and citing it here would be an overclaim.

*Resolution:* order-ops carries 6.7(A) as CORE and the Algebra I process standards A.1(A), A.1(D) and A.1(G). No §111.39 content citation is made, and this entry is why the build gate lets that pass.

### Not claimed at all

Deliberately NOT claimed at Level 1, so that a curriculum director can see the edges of the claim: A.2 in all its parts (writing linear equations in two variables), A.3 in all its parts including A.3(F) and A.3(G) (graphing linear functions and systems — Level 1 reads a supplied trace, it never produces one), A.4 (statistical fit), A.5(B) (linear inequalities), A.5(C) (systems), A.6 through A.9 (quadratics and exponentials), A.10(C), A.10(E), A.10(F) (division and factoring of trinomials and differences of squares), A.11 (radicals and laws of exponents), A.12(A), A.12(C), A.12(D) and A.12(E) (functions as objects, sequences, and literal equations).

## 6. What the game records as proof

The column called *evidence* in section 1 is not a count of questions answered. A mastery claim in ASCENT is made only when all of the following are true of one skill, and the progress report shows each of them separately for every claim:

1. **Posterior.** Bayesian Knowledge Tracing over unassisted responses puts the probability of knowing at **0.95** or above. Hinted and scaffolded successes move it fractionally and never satisfy the gate on their own.
2. **A clean run.** 3 unassisted correct responses in a row at difficulty band 3 or above.
3. **A proving run.** 3 consecutive unassisted items at band 4 or above, drawn from the item forms this learner has practised least, with worked-example support switched off. The run does not close until it has spanned two representations with at least one of them not symbolic, and has included one item that walks between a situation and the algebra.
4. **Prerequisites.** Every parent skill in the graph was already mastered before this one opened, so no claim rests on a foundation that was never checked.
5. **Retention.** The claim is re-probed on an expanding schedule of real elapsed time (), floored by  items of separation so a probe is never served twice inside one handful of questions. A miss makes the claim provisional; a second miss withdraws it and reopens practice. A mastery claim in this system can be taken back.

The difficulty bands those rules lean on are measured rather than asserted: `generators.demandOf()` scores every generated item and this build gate fails unless mean demand rises strictly from band 1 to band 5 within every skill.

The progress report surfaces all five, per skill, in English, Spanish and Polish, together with the count of claims that were later withdrawn on a retention probe — the **hollow-mastery rate**. It is shown rather than hidden, because a mastery system that cannot report its own false positives is not reporting anything.
